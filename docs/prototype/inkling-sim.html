Inkling kids mode

Click the device. Every screen is live. Tell me what's wrong with each one.

Kid home

Five things, nothing else. Read is the biggest tile and always first. Games show today's budget as a bar, not a number he can't read yet. "2 books today" is the only count he sees, and it resets tomorrow.

  • The gear needs a long press in the real app. Here it's a tap.
  • No status bar, no clock he can read, no notifications. Battery shown because he asks.
  • Andika typeface: designed for early readers, single-storey a and g, no confusable I/l.
  • Numbers like "12 of 30 min" and the clock are gone from his screen. He can't read them; the bar is the whole message.
  • Stars are gone (Jon's call, and the developmental psychologist's). Games unlock rule is off. Read tile first and biggest is the whole nudge.

Done for today

The cap doesn't interrupt a game mid-match. It shows up next time he taps the tile. Copy tells him when it's back and offers the one thing we want him to do instead.

Open questionDo you want a 5-minute warning inside the game, or is the gentle "next time" enough?

Shelf

Sorted by "what should he open next," not alphabetically. Each book gets one word: Just right, Stretch, Easy, Try it. That's the five-finger rule made visible: at his level, "just right" means about 1 unknown word in 20.

  • Decodables (Big Red Hen, Ship in the Shop) are short, pattern-controlled books we can write or generate. Trade books (Seuss, Pete) come from your library as PDFs.
  • "Easy" books stay. Re-reading favorites is where fluency comes from; we just don't push them. But "read 9 times" at 98% can be recitation, not decoding. The phonics map only trusts accuracy on books he hasn't seen before, or on novel lines inside generated decodables.
  • The shelf shows the 5 picks for today. "All books" opens the full library, sorted by level, so he can browse. The picks are the nudge; the library is the freedom.

Reader + tutor

One page, two buttons. "Read to me" is TTS with word highlighting. "I'll read" listens right here: tap it, the button turns live with a countdown, read the line, and the result lands on this same page. Tap it to see the simulated miscue on "pen" (short e is his shaky pattern), then tap again for the retry.

  • The coach never says "wrong." It shows the word in sounds with the tricky one marked. One miscue at a time; 3+ misses and the book quietly moves to "Stretch."
  • The real app stops listening when he stops talking (about 1.5 seconds of silence). The countdown is the ceiling, not the wait. Here it's just a visible stand-in.
  • Offline: on-device speech recognition, then a word diff against the page text. Kid speech is noisy, so only confident misses get flagged.
  • Logged: book, page, seconds, which button, words missed. That's the reading log and the phonics map in one stream.
  • Panel changes: "I'll read" is now the primary button and "Read to me" the fallback, so decoding is the default path. The coach speaks the sounds aloud, always. "Almost!" is gone; the coach just sounds the word out. Listening shows no countdown; the real app stops on silence (2.5 to 3 s for a 4-year-old, not 1.5).
  • Third tutor state, not shown here: when the recording is too quiet or the recognizer isn't confident, the coach says "I didn't catch that, try again" and never names a specific word. Guessing a wrong miscue is worse than saying nothing.
  • Typical 4-year-old articulation ("wed" for "red", "fin" for "thin") is not a decoding error and is never flagged.
  • A miss counts toward "too hard" only if it survives the retry, and every miss is logged with a confidence score so you can audit false flags.
The spike before any KotlinRecord Cove reading 20 decodable lines into the Boox mic in a normal room. Run offline recognition plus the diff. If more than 1 in 10 correct reads gets flagged as a miss, the coach needs redesign first.

PIN

Four digits. Any code works in the sim. In the real app this is also where kids mode gets turned off, so the whole parent side lives behind one gate.

Parent

One toggle at the top, everything else below it. Today: time by app. Reading: the phonics map and the words he missed twice. Next up: recommendations with the reason attached. Apps: every app installed on the device, one switch to put it on his home screen, one number for the daily cap. Rules: the read-first rule, quiet hours, PIN.

  • Apps is the whole customization story. Install anything from Play in parent mode, flip it on here. No config files, no chat.
  • The phonics map is the real product. Screen time is table stakes. The homeschool-parent persona said she'd look at the phonics map, the twice-missed words, and Next up, and ignore the minute bars.
  • Rules now has a whole-device daily ceiling (per-app caps alone could stack past 90 minutes), a 2-minute warning inside a game before its cap, and a Children row for a second profile. Profiles are phase 2 but the data model needs them from day 1.
  • Seed data is invented but shaped like a real 4-year-old at the Seuss level: CVC solid, short e wobbly, digraphs next.

Onboarding 1: profile

Name, age, a handful of interests as pictures-and-words chips. That's all the AI needs to make his first books. No email, no account, no reading-level dropdown for you to guess at.

Onboarding 2: placement

Six words, easy to hard, one per phonics stage: CVC, short e, sh digraph, silent e, vowel team. He reads what he can. The first stage he skips or misses is where the shelf starts. Two minutes, and it's the same speech pipeline as the tutor, so if this works the tutor works.

Onboarding 3: first shelf

Two sources. The starter pack ships inside the app: decodables for every stage, plus the Core Knowledge preschool and K read-alouds (nursery rhymes, Mother Goose, Aesop, the poems your kids already gravitate to). Text is tiny; it all fits. Then, on Wi-Fi, three books get generated from his interests at his exact stage. Pictures arrive a minute later.

  • Generated books are validated by a script before he sees them: every word must be decodable at his stage or on his sight-word list. The model doesn't get to freelance.
  • The "memory file" is per child: phonics map, missed words, books, interests. It's the only thing the model reads when it writes the next book.
Prototype only. No real speech, no real storage, no e-ink SDK. It exists so you can say "no, not like that" before any Kotlin gets written.
